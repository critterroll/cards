Randy Rejects

Click on any Card to save them

Each Critter can equip up to two items. Each item offers different perks and can alter your gameplay style.

Lawnbird

Great for beginners. Stat-wise, the Lawnbird is very well-rounded. It leans more toward offensive skills rather than defensive. If you’re new but like to take risks, this may be the critter for you.

The Lawnbird's power lets them inspect any place on the map. They do this by flying up to see the map through a bird’s eye view. But since they are ground birds, they must return to their original spot once the inspection is done.

Petrap

Small, but very durable, the Petrap is a very resilient critter. They're harder to beat than you think. They even waddle and scoot as a way to move. It can even jump through very bizarre methods that probably break several laws of physics. It uses its mouth as a hand, and its tie as a handy tool.

The Petrap can use its power to roll like a tumbling boulder and gain +4 on stamina. This allows it to move further in distance rolls, which can come in a lot of handy when you’re trying to get from one place to another.

Cat 'N' Mask

They wear masks for a bunch of reasons. These include fashion, culture, and protection from identity theft. Cats'n'Masks must be clever to outsmart their enemies. Thanks to their training in martial arts, they can meditate and gain much necessary wisdom. All three of their stats can be used for Stealthy encounters. If you desire to evade a 2nd round of an encounter, then be sure to take advantage of the cats' stats.

Their power grants them +2 on Smarts, which helps in Inspections and talk encounters.

Hobravin

These avian critters sadly can’t fly up to inspect the map like the Lawnbird. Thanks to its big brain that happens to be larger than its torso, they have high Smarts and Crafting, which can be great for finding AND making Items

Hobravins hold a very useful power. Without even rolling for craft, they can create health potions that raise 3 HP (no Item Card required if you roll successfully, just don't forget you have the power potion!).

Alanite

Alanites get their name from the Alacorn (a winged unicorn) and a chess knight. They also happen to be servants of a guy named Alan, but he’s been out of the picture for a while. Like the Petrap, the Alanite don’t have any arms or legs. But their wings grant them decent mobility.

Their >power grants them a finite form of telekinesis, which can move any object or critter from one room to another. There is nothing you can do to stop them when you’re being moved.

Snow Growl

Growls are ultimately just giant stuffed bears, but they prefer the term "Plush Golem." A diverse group, they are made of many different elements based on the various biomes they dwell in.

A Growl can stare into the eyes of any adjacent player and put them to sleep, resulting in a lost turn for the victim. This power can only be done to one player at a time. It can also be used for NPCs made by the game’s narrator. So it doesn't necessarily have to be a jerk move. The Snow Growl has a tendency to hide their sleeping victims (by covering the body with magazines, garbage, etc.). While their victims are asleep, nothing can be done to harm them.

Chewbunny

They must be cunning and quick to survive. Always be on the move and ready to work yourself out of a tricky situation.
Their sugar rush power gives them one advantage. After finishing a turn, the Chewbunny can exit the room they’re in to go to an adjacent space — as long as it's unlocked. Even if you've already rolled for distance, this power lets your critter move again, making the Chewbunny a great asset in stealth missions.

Chiplin

Chiplins are a hybrid of Gremlins and Chipmunks. They have high stats in Stealth, which is useful because one of the only times they can use their critter power is during a stealthful Action, Encounter, or Distance Roll.

Their power allows them to steal Item Cards, which is a move that NO ONE ELSE CAN DO. Despite this sounding like an overpowered move, they can only perform this act when unseen OR after winning a talk/physical encounter. Be sneaky and clever, and even the most powerful critters won’t see you coming.

Wookong

Heavy hitters and combat-oriented critters who also happen to be terrible at talking themselves out of fights.

Their power can absorb any damage or action done to them and then release it on their opponent. If someone inflicts the Wookong with a physical or talking encounter, the Wookong can turn the tables by doing the exact same action to the inflictor. For physical encounters, the exact number of damage done to them will be given to the victor if the Wookong lose. While this may seem like a crazy good power, it can’t be pocketed. They can only use it in real time.

Tr'elf

Tr'elfs are the only critters to really resemble people – because they’re technically not critters. These fawn-like beings were once rulers of the wild, but they stepped down from that position to be one with nature.

However, their long-lost royal insticts grant them +2 in Charisma, but it can only be tapped in by using their power This card is for players who prefer to be human-like characters in roleplaying games.

Trash-Dragon

Very sly creatures -- if interaction in roleplay games is boring for you, then this character is potentially great for you.
Trash-Dragons have the power to hide in their trash cans and totally disappear for the entirety of the player's turn. It’s great for avoiding potential encounters in the game. And even when they’re caught by a potential threat, they have Shield stats for extra protection.

Teiko

Wolves forever in puppy form. Teikos like to dress up as boxers because they think it’s funny. But if they wanted to be boxers (the fighters, not the dog breed), their stretchy arms give them great reach.

In fact, they can use their stretchy arm as their power to reach into adjacent open rooms. Here, they can interact with other critters or objects. They’ll even receive +1 in Athleticism. But most impressive of all, if they engage in encounters with critters in nearby rooms, their opponent can't retaliate.

Closemon

These closet monsters can take many forms due to their gaseous state. Anything with a lock is no problem for the Closemon!

And while Closemons can take damage and interact with physical objects, their power lets them momentarily phase through any locked areas or Location Cards. This comes into handy when traveling long distance. Your traveling won’t be halted short by having to unlock a room.

Insecktoid

When you're playing as an Insecktoid, be wary of groups that present a threat to you. If you can, find a group of friends you can trust. And if you’re ganged up on, it’s better to flee than to be a superhero. They have the lowest health of all the critters. Fortunately, they have really impressive stats with high Athleticism, Smarts, and Craft, making them the glass cannons of Critter Roll.

For their power, these sentient bots carry a special weapon that can immediately deal a confirmed 2HP damage to any critter present in their Location Card.

Mineytaur

Mineytaurs are the heaviest critter in the game, giving them high HP. They also have incredibly good stats for Craft and Athleticism… at a price. Since they don’t have hands - or hooves - they have a negative stat in Handiness. Don’t worry, this negative stat is generously compensated by a really cool power. Without having to roll for craft, the Mineytaur can build a Binding or Trap Item Card using their power.

If they start to carry too many items, they can make many friends by giving out their free items.

Deskmouse

The Deskmouse resembles a computer mouse, and that’s because they’re good at using computers. Deskmice stats shine in equipment operation on Location Cards. Even if the Narrator avoids adding machines to the Location Cards, the Deskmouse can still use Handiness to interact with its surroundings optimally.

Their power grants +3 on Craft. If saved up enough times, they can guarantee any Item Card when rolling for Craft.

Gelsen

Gelsens are intelligent blobs from an alien planet who came here to conquer all. But instead of that, they decided to just live their own life here. Their stats are the most active in the game with +2 on Athleticism and Charisma. The Gelsen can also shapeshift into many different forms. You'll never know which form you'll get.
For their power, their hands can morph into all sorts of tools that will grant +2 on Handiness. Like all stat-related powers, this ability can be pocketed and stacked for later use. Additionally, they can use this power to escape Binding.

Pair O' Pixies

The Pair o' Pixies aren't technically pixies. They’re sentient flames that take the form of tiny fairies. Canonically, these two are stuck together in the game. If you like to talk with yourself when playing, this can be a fun character. Just be sure to do different voices so players can discern who you’re speaking as. Their negative Athleticism should dissuade you from trying to get into encounters. If one pixie dies, the other dies.

For better odds, use the Pixies’ power to roll two times (and pick the highest roll). The roll to achieve this power can be attempted TWICE.

Widonome

The Widonome is a weed that walks, talks, and sees. The way these critters work is a little more unconventional than most. They have no stats that can help out in fights. Instead, Widonomes are great for alternate playstyles where players can think outside the box.

THEY HAVE TWO POWERS:
1. They can suck 2 HP from any Critter Card if it's on the same Location Card.
2. Their sneaky weed shenanigans can allow them to go underground, giving +2 on Stealth.

Pinatosaurus

The Piñatosaurus runs on candy, but don’t judge them for their tastes. These critters are incredibly intelligent who also know to fight. Unlike the Widnome, all of their stats help in encounters.

Their power is the ability to completely dodge any attack, even if the opponent rolls a natural 20 or uses their power. Because the Swing Dodge power can be saved and stacked, the Piñatosaurus can guarantee its own safety if attacked. This makes them more powerful overtime.

Osi

Nobody knows where the Osi came from, but they're here now and ready to make a difference. Many Osi's love to dress themselves up as very unique characters. They are pretty blank slates. If you play as an Osi, please draw as many features as you'd like onto them.

Their powers can be chosen by the player to replicate any Critter Power in the Critter Roll roster.
